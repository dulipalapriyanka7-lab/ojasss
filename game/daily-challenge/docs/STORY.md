# OJASSS Daily Challenge — Island Survival
## Story bible

---

## 1. The one-line pitch

**Every twenty minutes a ship drops twenty strangers on an island where bullets
don't kill — they rob. You leave with whatever Coins you are still holding when
the water comes.**

---

## 2. The world

Somewhere off the coast there is an island nobody owns and nobody governs. It
has four abandoned fishing villages, a few dirt tracks between them, palm
cover, and a shoreline that the sea takes back twice an hour.

Nobody lives there. People only *visit* — twenty at a time, for twenty minutes,
brought in by the same grey cargo ship with OJASSS stencilled on the hull. The
ship never stops running. It unloads, pulls back, waits ten minutes, and comes
again. It has been doing this all day and all night for as long as anyone can
remember, and no one has ever seen who gives the order.

The island is not dangerous in the way players expect. **Nobody dies there.**
That is the first thing every new player is told and the last thing they
believe.

---

## 3. The rule that makes the game

The guns on the island are real, and they hurt, but they do not take life.
They take **Coins**.

> When a bullet finds you, one Coin leaves your pocket and appears in the
> shooter's. You stay standing. You keep your gun. You keep playing.

This single change rewrites everything a player thinks they know:

- **There is no "last man standing."** Everyone who lands also leaves.
- **Losing is survivable, so nobody plays safe.** Death would make you hide.
  Losing one Coin makes you shoot back.
- **A fight is never finished.** The player you just robbed is still behind
  you, still armed, still counting.
- **The leader is the target.** Your Coin balance floats over your head for
  everyone to read. Getting rich paints a sign on your back.

Every player boards the ship with the **same 50 Coins**. Nothing is awarded for
free. The only Coins on the island are the ones the twenty of you brought —
plus whatever the Horse is carrying.

---

## 4. The island's four beats

A round is twenty minutes and has four movements, and every player learns to
feel them without looking at the clock.

### Beat 1 — The drop (00:00)
The ship cuts its engines off the western coast. Twenty people go over the side
and come down across the island, far enough apart that the first two minutes
are quiet. You find cover. You check your ammunition. You wonder who landed
near you.

### Beat 2 — The hunt (00:00 → 18:00)
Open season. Find people, shoot people, take their Coins. But the island is
large and twenty people are easy to lose, so the island does something about
that:

> **Every two minutes, the island shows you everybody.**
>
> For a few seconds, every player's position burns onto the map. Hunters see
> their prey. Prey sees the hunters. Hiding buys you two minutes and no more.

Nine times in a round, the map lights up. Nine times, the whole island decides
again who is chasing whom. Between pings, the smart players move — because the
position the island showed is the position everyone is walking towards.

### Beat 3 — The storm and the Horse (18:00)
Two minutes left. The sky closes. Rain comes in sideways and the sea starts
climbing the beach, swallowing the coast metre by metre and pushing everyone
who is still alive — and everyone is still alive — into a smaller and smaller
circle of dry land.

And out of the rain comes the **Treasure Horse**.

A golden animal, unbothered by the weather, carrying **500 Coins** visible on
its flank for everyone to count. It does not fight. It does not run from you.
It simply walks the shrinking island while twenty desperate people shoot at it,
and every single hit knocks one Coin loose for whoever pulled the trigger.

The Horse is the great equaliser. The player sitting on 90 Coins and the player
down to 4 are suddenly shooting at the same target, side by side, in the rain,
on a strip of land the sea is eating. When the last of its 500 Coins is gone,
the Horse walks back into the storm — and returns, moments later, loaded again.

There is no buried chest. There is no secret. **There is only the Horse, and
there are only two minutes.**

### Beat 4 — The count (20:00)
The water stops. The guns stop. Everyone's Coins are counted exactly as they
stand, and the board is posted: #1, #2, #3, and your own line somewhere in the
twenty.

Then the ship comes back, ten minutes later, and does it all again.

---

## 5. The feeling we are selling

| Minute | What the player should be feeling |
|---|---|
| 00:00 | "Where is everyone?" — quiet, scanning, a little exposed |
| 02:00 | First ping. "Three of them are *right there*." |
| 04:00–16:00 | A rolling brawl. Coins going both ways. Nobody out. |
| 16:00 | Checking the balance. Doing arithmetic. Getting greedy. |
| 18:00 | Sky goes black. **"Horse."** Everyone runs the same direction. |
| 18:00–20:00 | Total chaos in the rain on shrinking ground |
| 20:00 | The count. Relief or revenge. |
| 20:00–30:00 | Ten minutes to tell someone what happened. |

The round is designed so that **the last two minutes can rewrite the whole
leaderboard**. A player who spent eighteen minutes losing can still take fifty
Coins off the Horse and finish in the top three. That is the hook that makes
people wait through the break.

---

## 6. The ship, the break and the "always on" promise

The ship is the game's clock, and the clock is the product.

> **You never wait for a game to be organised. You wait for the next ship.**

Twenty minutes on, ten minutes off, forty-eight rounds a day, no admin, no
lobby host, no "waiting for players." Open the app at 3 a.m. and a round is
either running or starting in under ten minutes. The ten-minute break is not
dead time — it is the window where you see the board, see what the Horse paid
out, and register for the next ship.

The promise, in the player's words:

- **ఎవరైనా, ఎప్పుడైనా వచ్చి ఆడవచ్చు** — anyone can turn up and play.
- **ప్రతి 20 నిమిషాలకు కొత్త Game** — a new game every twenty minutes.
- **Admin అవసరం లేదు** — nobody has to start it.

---

## 7. Characters and voice

**The twenty.** Not heroes. Ordinary players with ordinary names — Player01,
RaviK, Sunitha, SaiTeja — whose only identity in the round is a name tag and a
Coin count hovering over their head. The game's whole social layer is that
number: it tells you who is winning, who is desperate, and who is worth
shooting.

**The Treasure Horse.** The only non-player character in the game, and it never
speaks, never attacks, never flees. It is pure temptation with legs. Give it
weight: golden, slow, impossible to miss, glowing faintly through the rain. It
is the only thing on the island that *gives* instead of taking.

**The ship.** Silent and punctual. It is the hand of the schedule. Players
should come to read its arrival as "the round is real now."

**Tone.** Bright, loud, Telugu-first, arcade — not grim survival horror.
Nobody dies here. It is a twenty-minute heist with guns, and the loudest
emotion on screen should be greed, not fear.

---

## 8. Why each rule exists (keep these when the design changes)

| Rule | What it protects |
|---|---|
| Hits steal Coins, never eliminate | Every one of the twenty plays all twenty minutes — no spectating, no churn |
| Everyone starts on equal Coins | No pay-to-win, no veteran advantage at the drop |
| Coin balance shown over every head | Makes the leader a target and keeps the field level |
| Location ping every 2 minutes | Kills camping and guarantees contact on a big map |
| Storm + Horse only in the last 2 min | Puts the biggest swing at the end, so nobody leaves early |
| Horse refills and returns | The finale never runs dry, however fast the Coins are taken |
| Fixed 20 + 10 forever | The product is "always on"; a schedule is easier to trust than a queue |
| No secret chest | One finale objective, which the whole island can see and fight over |

---

## 9. First version scope

Per the brief: **ship the demo prototype first.** One island, one weapon, bots
filling the twenty, the full 20 + 10 cycle, the ping, the storm and the Horse.

Voice chat is explicitly **not** in the first version — the rules for it (who
can hear whom, whether it is team-wide or proximity, how it is moderated)
haven't been decided yet, and it should be added only once they are.
